# The new first 20 levels

The goal is a calm opening with visible cause and effect: match boxes, make room, prepare a chain, then plan ahead. The game keeps the same tap controls and parking-based stars. Early teaching levels allow the intended lesson without taking away a star for following it.

Levels 1–3 introduce the loop. Levels 4–9 alternate planning and relief. Levels 10–12 widen the colour mix and queue. Levels 13–15 introduce covered cargo with a breather afterwards. Levels 16–18 teach locks; 19–20 combine familiar ideas.

The design aims for the four covered levels to be playable using only visible information. The included verifier replays eight stored variant-specific solutions; it does not independently verify an observation-based strategy. Chain opportunities fit within the three-star allowance where they are offered; levels 4, 6 and 7 are not marked as chain-capable in the stored pack.

The most valuable next test is watching new players through levels 1–5, 10, 13 and 16 on a phone: can they explain parking and chains, read empty blue/green decks, and predict the result of a tap without coaching? Then tune pacing from observed mistakes and hesitation. Simulation clear rates are not player difficulty measurements.
